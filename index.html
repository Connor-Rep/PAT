Regulations & Information

What the law requires and what testing involves

The Electricity At Work Regulations

These regulations place a legal responsibility on employers and employees, including self-employed people, to comply and take reasonably practicable steps to make sure no danger comes from using electrical equipment. In practice that means having a programme of inspection and electrical safety testing of portable appliances in place, carried out routinely by a competent person.

The Health and Safety at Work Act

This puts a duty of care on both employer and employee to ensure the safety of everyone using the premises, including where:

  • Appliances are used by employees
  • The public use electrical appliances, such as colleges, schools, hospitals, gyms, hotels and B&Bs
  • Electrical appliances are supplied or hired
  • Electrical appliances are repaired or serviced

What does testing involve?

Our engineers carry out PAT testing in line with the IEE Code of Practice and HSE guidelines. Every appliance gets a thorough visual check of its casing, flex and plug, looking for damage, wear, correct wiring and the right fuse rating.

Once an appliance passes its visual inspection, our engineer performs a series of electrical tests using a PAT testing unit. We test 240V, 110V and 415V equipment connected to the mains via a plug.

On completion, you receive a detailed test report and certificate confirming your equipment is safe to use.

Where we test

Offices, schools, factories, nurseries, nightclubs, cafes, hotels, hairdressers and barbers, landlords, gymnasiums: any premises that needs to comply with Health & Safety legislation.

Why it protects you

Insurers assume a business is complying with all necessary regulations when providing cover, and may refuse or reduce a claim for damage caused by an untested appliance. Regular PAT testing gives employers and landlords the evidence they need.
